  \begin{abstract}
    The Lorentz--capacity formulation carries an open bridge, LC-O2-O1: the equivalence between the
    capacity radius $R^{2}=1-\epsilon$ and the metric radius defined by the temporal lapse.
    A proposed closure reduces the local temporal coefficient to the free capacity fraction,
    $A_{\tau}=A_{\tau}^{\infty}(1-\epsilon)$.
    This note shows that the proposal fails.
    Assuming a diagonal temporal block, this law together with the principal-symbol identification
    $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$ makes the metric radius increase with occupancy,
    $R_{\mathrm{metric}}^{2}=1/(1-\epsilon)$, so the two definitions agree only at $\epsilon=0$; no
    constant renormalisation repairs this.
    What survives is the co-metric statement $g^{\tau\tau}/g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}=1-\epsilon$, the
    metric inverse of the lapse, which does not govern proper-time dilation.
    Two further defects are independent of the inversion: the partial-trace form of the coefficient is
    a fresh postulate, and the occupancy is defined by the norm of a vector while the trace step
    treats it as a subspace.
    The result is narrow --- a reciprocal scalar law would close the bridge algebraically --- so what
    fails is the free-fraction law, not scalar closure in general.
    The no-go holds for any postulated flat value $A_{\tau}^{\infty}$, which no source fixes; LC-O2-O1 is open.
    Interpretively, and as no part of the formal result, capacity appears to constrain the co-metric
    more directly than the lapse.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:introduction}

    The Lorentz-capacity formulation~\cite{Beau2026lco2} introduces a local occupancy field
    $\epsilon(x)$ measuring how much of the projective capacity at $x$ is already locked by admissible
    spectral structure, and defines the residual capacity radius by
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Rcap}
      R_{\mathrm{capacity}}(x)^{2} := 1-\epsilon(x).
    \end{equation}
    Independently, it defines a metric radius from the temporal lapse, normalised to its flat-space
    value,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Rmet}
      R_{\mathrm{metric}}(x)^{2}
      := \frac{g_{\tau\tau}(x)}{g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}},
      \qquad
      g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=-\frac{1}{A_{\tau}^{\infty}} ,
    \end{equation}
    where $A_{\tau}^{\infty}>0$ is a postulated flat-space value of the temporal coefficient.
    Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} shows that spatial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ invariance leaves that coefficient free, so no
    source fixes $A_{\tau}^{\infty}$; the normalisation $g^{\mu\nu}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$, in which
    $A_{\tau}^{\infty}=2$ and $g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=-\tfrac12$, is a choice of units.
    The open problem LC-O2-O1 (Remark~3.2 and Section~7.3 of~\cite{Beau2026lco2}) is to prove that
    these two radii coincide, from admissibility data alone, without importing the Einstein equation
    from the spectral gravity branch.

    A proposed closure uses the local weight reduction
    $A_{\tau}(x)=A_{\tau}^{\infty}(1-\epsilon(x))$ --- here called the \emph{free-fraction law} ---
    together with a principal-symbol identification $g^{\tau\tau}(x)=-A_{\tau}(x)/4$.
    The argument does not establish LC-O2-O1.

    The factor $\tfrac14$ is not read off any principal symbol; it is fixed so
    that the two definitions agree, using, in the normalisation $A_{\tau}^{\infty}=2$, the flat-space assignment
    $g^{\tau\tau,\infty}=-\tfrac12$.
    That assignment is the value of the covariant component $g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=-1/A_{\tau}^{\infty}$, not of
    the contravariant $g^{\tau\tau,\infty}=-A_{\tau}^{\infty}$, which is $-2$ in that normalisation.
    With the correct identification $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$, the free-fraction law delivers the
    reciprocal of~\eqref{eq:Rcap} rather than~\eqref{eq:Rcap}.

    \paragraph{Scope of the negative result.}
      The result proved here is narrow by design.
      It concerns the \emph{free-fraction} law specifically, not scalar closure in general: a
      reciprocal scalar law $A_{\tau}(\epsilon)=A_{\tau}^{\infty}/(1-\epsilon)$ would close the bridge
      algebraically.
      No such law is derived here, and none is available in the corpus; but its mere existence means
      that what fails is a particular reduction, not the possibility of a scalar one.
      We likewise do not claim that an operator-valued datum is \emph{necessary}, nor that a
      covariantly transforming family of local subspaces is impossible, nor that the spatial
      components must be derived jointly in order to close the temporal bridge alone.
      Those remain open questions, listed in Section~\ref{sec:remaining}.

    Section~\ref{sec:nogo} states the inversion as a no-go under an explicit diagonality hypothesis.
    Section~\ref{sec:renorm} shows that no available renormalisation repairs it, and isolates the
    co-metric statement that does survive.
    Section~\ref{sec:provenance} shows that the trace reduction rests on an unsupported attribution and
    on a type mismatch.
    Section~\ref{sec:covariance} states what is actually missing on the equivariance side.
    Sections~\ref{sec:status} and~\ref{sec:remaining} record the status and the open content.

  \section{The scalar free-fraction ansatz}
    \label{sec:occupancy}

    We retain the definitions of the occupancy field; what fails is the inference drawn from them.

    Let the local rank-three effective sector be the spin-one module
    $H_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)\simeq \Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})$, with $V_{\rho}$ the supplied spin-$\tfrac12$ carrier, equipped
    with its $\SU(2)$-invariant quadratic norm; this is the module that Q7 maps equivariantly to the complexified
    spatial sector, as recorded in Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11}.

    \begin{definition}[Local quadratic occupancy]
      \label{def:local-occupancy}
      Let $C_{\mathrm{occ}}(x)$ denote the occupied part of the local admissible sector over $x$, and
      let $C_{\mathrm{max}}(x)$ denote a maximally saturated admissible vector, with
      \[
        \sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
        :=\|C_{\mathrm{max}}(x)\|_{\Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})}^{2}
        =c_{\mathrm{BI}}^{2}.
      \]
      The local occupancy field is
      \[
        \epsilon(x)
        :=\frac{\|C_{\mathrm{occ}}(x)\|_{\Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}},
        \qquad 0\leq\epsilon(x)< 1 .
      \]
    \end{definition}

    \begin{definition}[Free-fraction law]
      \label{def:ansatz}
      The \emph{free-fraction law} is the hypothesis
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:ansatz}
        A_{\tau}(x)=A_{\tau}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\epsilon(x)\bigr),
        \qquad A_{\tau}^{\infty}>0 \text{ postulated}.
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    \begin{definition}[Diagonal temporal block]
      \label{def:diagonal}
      We say the temporal block is \emph{diagonal} at $x$ if $g^{\tau i}(x)=0$ for the spatial indices
      $i$.
      In that case, and only in that case, the temporal components satisfy
      $g_{\tau\tau}(x)=\bigl(g^{\tau\tau}(x)\bigr)^{-1}$.
    \end{definition}

    Diagonality holds for the homogeneous form of Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11}, Corollary~6.1, which has no mixed
    temporal--spatial term, and in the static configurations considered in~\cite{Beau2026lco2}.
    It is stated explicitly because the inversion below uses it; without it the relation between
    $g^{\tau\tau}$ and $g_{\tau\tau}$ involves the full inverse of the metric and no elementary
    reciprocal holds.

  \section{The no-go for the free-fraction law}
    \label{sec:nogo}

    Under the hypotheses of its Corollary~6.1 ([H-L] and [H-lift], a supplied full-rank extension satisfying
    [T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8, and invariance under [ACT$_\tau$] with a negative ordering term),
    Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} gives the homogeneous co-metric in the form
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:q11}
      g^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-A_{\tau},\,2\lambda,\,2\lambda,\,2\lambda),
      \qquad
      A_{\tau}>0,\ \lambda>0 \text{ independent},
    \end{equation}
    and shows that spatial $\SU(2)$ invariance fixes neither $A_{\tau}$ nor its ratio to $\lambda$; the Casimir value
    $2$ is a choice of units, not a consequence of invariance.
    The temporal entry is read off the principal symbol, $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$ (Q5b Theorem~5.2, Q11
    Theorem~1.1(c)), so $g^{\tau\tau,\infty}=-A_{\tau}^{\infty}$ and
    $g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=-1/A_{\tau}^{\infty}$ with $A_{\tau}^{\infty}>0$ postulated.

    \begin{theorem}[Failure of the free-fraction closure]
      \label{thm:nogo}
      Assume the free-fraction law~\eqref{eq:ansatz} with any postulated $A_{\tau}^{\infty}>0$, the
      principal-symbol identification $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$, and a diagonal temporal block
      (Definition~\ref{def:diagonal}).
      Then
      \[
        R_{\mathrm{metric}}(x)^{2}=\frac{1}{1-\epsilon(x)} ,
      \]
      and therefore
      \[
        R_{\mathrm{metric}}(x)^{2}=R_{\mathrm{capacity}}(x)^{2}
        \iff (1-\epsilon(x))^{2}=1
        \iff \epsilon(x)=0
      \]
      in the physical range $0\le\epsilon(x)<1$.
      The free-fraction law thus closes the bridge on the homogeneous vacuum only.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      By~\eqref{eq:ansatz} and~\eqref{eq:q11},
      $g^{\tau\tau}(x)=-A_{\tau}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\epsilon(x)\bigr)$.
      Diagonality gives
      \[
        g_{\tau\tau}(x)=\bigl(g^{\tau\tau}(x)\bigr)^{-1}=-\bigl[A_{\tau}^{\infty}(1-\epsilon(x))\bigr]^{-1}.
      \]
      Dividing by $g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=-1/A_{\tau}^{\infty}$ yields
      $R_{\mathrm{metric}}(x)^{2}=1/(1-\epsilon(x))$; the value of $A_{\tau}^{\infty}$ cancels.
      Equating with $1-\epsilon(x)$ gives $(1-\epsilon(x))^{2}=1$, whose only root with
      $0\le\epsilon<1$ is $\epsilon=0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[A reciprocal law would close it]
      \label{rem:reciprocal}
      The obstruction is specific to the free-fraction form.
      Under the same hypotheses, the reciprocal scalar law
      $A_{\tau}(\epsilon)=A_{\tau}^{\infty}/(1-\epsilon)$ gives
      $g_{\tau\tau}/g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}=1-\epsilon$, closing the bridge algebraically.
      We do not derive such a law, and none is available in the corpus; adopting it merely because it
      produces the required identity would select the mechanism from the desired conclusion.
      Its existence nevertheless bounds the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}: what fails is the
      free-fraction law, not scalar closure as such.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Metric--co-metric mismatch]
      \label{rem:where}
      The proposed derivation writes the metric definition contravariantly, as
      $R^{2}=-2g^{\tau\tau}$, whereas
      \cite{Beau2026lco2} defines it covariantly, as
      $R^{2}=g_{\tau\tau}/g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}$, which is $-2g_{\tau\tau}$ in the normalisation
      $A_{\tau}^{\infty}=2$.
      It then fixes $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}/4$ by imposing $g^{\tau\tau,\infty}=-\tfrac12$, which in that
      normalisation is the covariant reference value.
      The factor $\tfrac14$ reconciles the two definitions numerically at the cost of conflating the
      metric with the co-metric.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Direction of the physical discrepancy]
      \label{rem:physical}
      The discrepancy is one of direction, not of convention.
      Proper time obeys $d\tau_{\mathrm{proper}}=\sqrt{|g_{\tau\tau}|}\,dt$, so a coefficient
      $A_{\tau}$ decreasing with occupancy makes $|g_{\tau\tau}|$ increase, and local clocks run
      faster near an occupied region.
      A Schwarzschild exterior requires the opposite: there $|g_{\tau\tau}|$ decreases with
      $r_{s}/r$, so $A_{\tau}$ must increase with occupancy.
    \end{remark}

  \section{No available renormalisation repairs the inversion}
    \label{sec:renorm}

    \begin{proposition}[Constant renormalisation is inert]
      \label{prop:constant-renorm}
      Let $\widetilde g^{\mu\nu}=\kappa\,g^{\mu\nu}$ with $\kappa>0$ constant.
      Then
      \[
        \frac{\widetilde g^{\tau\tau}(x)}{\widetilde g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}}
        =\frac{g^{\tau\tau}(x)}{g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}},
        \qquad
        \frac{\widetilde g_{\tau\tau}(x)}{\widetilde g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}}
        =\frac{g_{\tau\tau}(x)}{g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}} .
      \]
      In particular, from $A_{\tau}^{\infty}=2$, $\kappa=\tfrac14$ reproduces the flat-space value
      $\widetilde g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}=-\tfrac12$ used in the proposed derivation while leaving
      Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} intact.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Numerator and denominator scale by $\kappa$ in the contravariant ratio, and by $\kappa^{-1}$ in
      the covariant ratio since $\widetilde g_{\tau\tau}=\kappa^{-1}g_{\tau\tau}$.
      The factor cancels in each case.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[A local conformal factor would beg the question]
      \label{rem:local-renorm}
      A position-dependent factor $\Omega^{2}(x)$ could force agreement, but only for
      $\Omega^{2}(x)\propto\bigl(1-\epsilon(x)\bigr)^{-2}$, a law read off the desired conclusion.
      It would also rescale the spatial components, and it appears in no source paper.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[No densitised co-metric is defined]
      \label{rem:densitised}
      Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} identifies the principal symbol as
      $\sigma_{2}(L_{\mathrm{eff}})(p,k)=g^{\mu\nu}(p)\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}$, leaving a global scale
      freedom, which Proposition~\ref{prop:constant-renorm} shows to be inert.
      No variable measure or dualisation supplying the missing factor is defined in the corpus.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[What the free-fraction law does support]
      \label{prop:co-ansatz}
      Under~\eqref{eq:ansatz} and $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$,
      \[
        R_{\mathrm{co}}(x)^{2}
        :=\frac{g^{\tau\tau}(x)}{g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}}
        =1-\epsilon(x),
      \]
      exactly.
      With a diagonal temporal block, $R_{\mathrm{co}}^{2}=1/R_{\mathrm{metric}}^{2}$, so
      $R_{\mathrm{co}}$ is the metric inverse of the capacity lapse and does not govern proper-time
      dilation.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The first identity is immediate.
      The second follows from $g_{\tau\tau}=(g^{\tau\tau})^{-1}$ under
      Definition~\ref{def:diagonal}.
    \end{proof}

    The two statements to distinguish are therefore
    \begin{align*}
      \frac{g^{\tau\tau}}{g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}}&=1-\epsilon
        &&\text{(holds under the free-fraction law)},\\[2pt]
      \frac{g_{\tau\tau}}{g_{\tau\tau}^{\infty}}&=1-\epsilon
        &&\text{(the open bridge).}
    \end{align*}

  \section{Provenance and typing of the trace reduction}
    \label{sec:provenance}

    \begin{proposition}[The trace form is a fresh postulate]
      \label{prop:provenance}
      Neither~\cite{Beau2026w1} nor~\cite{Beau2026q11} establishes that the local temporal coefficient
      of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is a partial trace over an available fibre sector.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      W1~\cite{Beau2026w1} defines the admissibility weights as cumulative fingerprint capacities,
      $a_{q}(s)=\sum_{n\le n^{*}}\sigma_{c(s)}(n)$, and establishes their stabilisation as weights of a
      filtered Dirichlet form.
      A Dirichlet weight is a coefficient multiplying squared increments, not a partial trace, and W1
      states no dependence on any occupancy field.
      Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} determines no value of $A_{\tau}$: spatial $\SU(2)$ invariance leaves it free, and
      its Casimir value $2$ is a choice of units.
      Neither a free invariant scalar nor a Casimir eigenvalue is a normalised trace over a variable subspace.
      Neither source therefore supplies the asserted form, which is an independent hypothesis.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[Type mismatch: vector versus subspace]
      \label{prop:type}
      Definition~\ref{def:local-occupancy} measures occupancy by the norms of \emph{vectors}
      $C_{\mathrm{occ}}(x),C_{\mathrm{max}}(x)\in\Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})$, whereas the partial-trace step of
      the partial-trace construction treats them as \emph{subspaces}.
      For a subspace $C$ and a weight acting as $\lambda\cdot\mathrm{Id}$,
      \[
        \tr_{C}(\lambda\,\mathrm{Id})=\lambda\dim C ,
      \]
      so a ratio of such traces is quantised in $\{0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1\}$, since
      $\dim\Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})=3$, while $\epsilon(x)$ ranges continuously over $[0,1)$.
      The two coincide at finitely many points only.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The trace of a scalar operator over a subspace $C$ is $\lambda\dim C$ by definition of the trace
      on $C$; the stated range follows.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What a corrected statement would require]
      \label{rem:type-fix}
      To give a norm-valued occupancy a meaning at the level of traces, one must introduce a further
      object --- an occupation operator, a density matrix, or a weighted projector --- whose trace
      reproduces the ratio of Definition~\ref{def:local-occupancy}.
      No such object is defined in the construction or in the cited sources.
      This defect is independent of Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}: it would persist even if the monotonicity
      were correct.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[What Schur's lemma gives]
      \label{rem:schur}
      Applied to the irreducible module $\Sym^{2}(V_{\rho})$, Schur's lemma forces an equivariant
      admissibility weight to act as $\lambda\cdot\mathrm{Id}$.
      That is all it gives: \emph{scalarity} of the weight.
      It determines neither which functional of the local sector the principal-symbol coefficient is,
      nor the dependence of that coefficient on $\epsilon$, nor its monotonicity.
      The closure argument reads a determination of the coefficient into a statement about scalarity.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The missing local covariance law}
    \label{sec:covariance}

    The free-fraction construction restricts the admissibility weight to a subspace
    $C_{\mathrm{free}}(x)$.
    A proper subspace of an irreducible module is not invariant, so such a restriction is not by itself
    an equivariant operation.

    This does not make a local subspace field illegitimate.
    A family $C_{\mathrm{free}}(x)$ may transform covariantly under the local action, and invariant
    scalars --- its rank, or the norm of a distinguished vector --- may then be well defined.
    No such covariance law is supplied: no transformation rule for
    $C_{\mathrm{free}}(x)$ is stated, and without one the homogeneous form of~\cite{Beau2026q11}, which rests on
    equivariance and fixes no value of $A_{\tau}^{\infty}$, cannot be transported to the local coefficient.

    \begin{remark}[Status of this point]
      \label{rem:covariance-status}
      The obstruction is therefore an absence, not an impossibility: constructing a covariantly
      transforming local sector, with an explicit rule allowing the homogeneous form of Q11 to be applied
      pointwise, is one of
      the open routes listed in Section~\ref{sec:remaining}.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Status of LC-O2-O1}
    \label{sec:status}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item The free-fraction law does \textbf{not} close LC-O2-O1, for any postulated
        $A_{\tau}^{\infty}>0$, and the identity $\epsilon(x)=1+2g^{\tau\tau}(x)$ of the proposed derivation does
        not follow.
      \item The partial-trace form does \textbf{not} follow from~\cite{Beau2026w1}
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:provenance}), and the trace step has a type mismatch
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:type}).
      \item What \textbf{survives} is the co-metric statement
        $g^{\tau\tau}/g^{\tau\tau}_{\infty}=1-\epsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:co-ansatz}); it is not
        the capacity lapse and does not yield time dilation.
      \item \textbf{LC-O2-O1 is open.} Remark~3.2 of~\cite{Beau2026lco2} states the equivalence as an
        explicitly declared postulate, physically coherent and consistent with a supplied Schwarzschild
        weak-field metric, but not derived.
      \item The results of~\cite{Beau2026lco2} that take $R(x)$ as given --- the local Born--Infeld
        sphere, the fibrewise temporal residual map, and the gravitational time-dilation corollary ---
        are \textbf{unaffected}.
      \item The negative result is \textbf{narrow}: it refutes the free-fraction law, not scalar
        closure in general (Remark~\ref{rem:reciprocal}).
    \end{itemize}

  \section{Open content}
    \label{sec:remaining}

    The following are open, and are listed as candidate routes rather than as established
    requirements.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Monotonicity.} Any closure must make the temporal co-metric coefficient increase
        where capacity is consumed (Remark~\ref{rem:physical}), and this should follow from the
        construction rather than be imposed on it.
        Whether a derived scalar law with this property exists is open
        (Remark~\ref{rem:reciprocal}).
      \item \textbf{Local covariance.} A transformation rule for the local admissible sector, allowing
        the homogeneous form of~\cite{Beau2026q11} to be applied pointwise, is missing
        (Section~\ref{sec:covariance}).
      \item \textbf{Typing of the occupancy.} If the occupancy is to enter through a trace, the
        corresponding occupation operator or weighted projector must be defined
        (Remark~\ref{rem:type-fix}).
      \item \textbf{Candidate: an operator-valued datum.} One candidate for the datum controlling the
        coefficient is the local projector $P_{\mathrm{free}}(x)$, with the reduction derived rather
        than posited.
        We do not claim that a scalar datum is insufficient; this is one route among others.
      \item \textbf{Relation to the spatial components.} Reproducing a Schwarzschild exterior requires
        $g^{\tau\tau}$ and $g^{rr}$ to vary differently.
        Whether closing the temporal bridge LC-O2-O1 alone requires a joint temporal--spatial law is
        not settled here.
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The bridge LC-O2-O1 is not closed by the free-fraction law.
    Under that law together with the principal-symbol identification $g^{\tau\tau}=-A_{\tau}$ and a diagonal
    temporal block, for any postulated flat-space value $A_{\tau}^{\infty}$, the metric radius varies as $1/(1-\epsilon)$ while the capacity radius varies as
    $1-\epsilon$; the two coincide only in the homogeneous vacuum
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}).
    No available renormalisation repairs this (Section~\ref{sec:renorm}).
    The closure construction additionally rests on a partial-trace form that its cited source does not
    supply (Proposition~\ref{prop:provenance}) and that has a type mismatch
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:type}).

    The failure is one of direction rather than of normalisation: a free-fraction law moves the
    temporal coefficient the wrong way.
    The postulate of~\cite{Beau2026lco2} remains available and physically coherent.
    This result identifies why the free-fraction construction fails and clarifies the surviving
    co-metric statement.

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
      Beyond the formal content, and without claiming it as a result, the pattern is suggestive: the
      quantity that the free-fraction law controls cleanly is the co-metric ratio, not the lapse.
      If local capacity constrains the co-metric more directly than the proper-time lapse, then the
      capacity language may be better suited to the symbol side of the correspondence than to the
      metric side, and a closure of LC-O2-O1 would have to pass through an explicit inversion step
      rather than around it.
      This reading is interpretive and forms no part of Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}.
